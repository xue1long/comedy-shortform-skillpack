﻿# Sitcom Episode Structure

## R — Reading
Episode structure is about restoring balance after disruption. The disruption is the comedy; the restoration is the resolution. Unlike film (transformation), sitcom is about the *attempt* to restore — the characters try to fix the problem and make it worse.

## I — Interpretation
The episode works because the audience wants the characters to fail to change — the comedy is in the failed restoration. The episode fails when the characters actually solve the problem (that's drama, not sitcom).

## A1 — Application
For shortform: the episode is compressed. Establish the problem (0-10s) → attempt to fix (10-45s) → new stability (45-60s). The attempt is the comedy; the new stability is the payoff.

## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Structure this sitcom episode" - "Write a sitcom episode" - "How do I structure this?"

## E — Executable steps
1. Establish the problem (the disruption).
2. Show the characters trying to fix it (and making it worse).
3. Reach the new stability (the resolution).
4. Check: the new stability must be different from the old — or the same but with the audience wiser.
5. Verify: the comedy is in the failed attempt, not the success.

## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: episode that's about solving the problem — sitcom is about the attempt, not the solution.
- **Trap**: resolution that's too neat — the new stability must be slightly different.
- **Trap**: characters who actually change — sitcom characters are static.
