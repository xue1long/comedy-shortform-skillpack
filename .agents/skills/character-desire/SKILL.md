﻿# Character Desire Engine
## R — Reading
Corbett's engine: character desire = want vs need. The want is what the character pursues consciously; the need is what they actually require (often opposite). The gap between want and need is the drama — the character pursues what they want and discovers what they need. Desire is not a trait; it's the engine that produces behavior.
## I — Interpretation
Desire works because it gives the character agency — they act, not just react. The want/need gap produces growth: the character gets what they wanted and discovers it wasn't what they needed. The desire fails when it's vague ("be happy") or when want and need are the same.
## A1 — Application
For shortform: write the character's want in one sentence (specific, immediate). The need is often the opposite — write it separately. The story is the collision between want and need.
## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Design a character"
- "This character has no drive"
- "What does this character want?"
## E — Executable steps
1. Write the character's want in one sentence (specific, immediate).
2. Write the character's need in one sentence (often opposite to want).
3. Check: do want and need collide?
4. Write the scene where the character pursues the want.
5. The payoff: they get what they wanted and discover the need.
## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: want that's vague — "be happy" has no engine.
- **Trap**: want and need identical — no gap means no drama.
- **Trap**: desire that's static — the character must pursue, not just want.
