﻿# Character Contradiction
## R — Reading
Contradiction is the engine of depth: a character who contains opposites. Corbett's model: contradictory traits produce surprise because the audience can't predict which trait will dominate. The contradiction must be *organic* — both traits are true; neither is a mask. Example: a ruthless businessman who weeps at opera; a coward who fights when it matters.
## I — Interpretation
Contradiction works because it produces surprise — the audience predicts behavior from one trait and is surprised by the other. The contradiction fails when it's decorative (traits that don't interact) or when it's a mask (one trait is the "real" one).
## A1 — Application
For shortform: identify two opposing traits in your character. Write a scene where each trait produces a different reaction — the comedy is the audience's surprise at the collision.
## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Make this character deeper"
- "This character is one-note"
- "Add contradiction"
## E — Executable steps
1. Identify the character's dominant trait.
2. Identify its opposite (or a trait that collides with it).
3. Check: are both traits true (not a mask)?
4. Write a scene where each trait produces a different action.
5. The comedy is the audience's surprise at the collision.
## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: contradiction that's decorative — traits that don't interact.
- **Trap**: mask contradiction — one trait is the "real" one.
- **Trap**: contradiction that's too neat — the surprise is when it's NOT neat.
