# Sitcom Arc

## R — Reading
Vorhaus's sitcom arc: old stability → instability → new stability. The disruption is the comedy; the restoration is the resolution. Unlike film (transformation), sitcom is about restoring the status quo — the audience wants the characters to fail to change.

## I — Interpretation
The arc works because sitcom is about *stability*, not change. The comedy comes from the disruption of stability — the characters try to restore the status quo and fail, or succeed in a way that creates a new status quo.

## A1 — Application
For shortform: the arc is compressed. Old stability (0-10s) → disruption (10-45s) → new stability (45-60s). The disruption is the comedy; the new stability is the payoff.

## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Write a sitcom episode" - "Structure this sitcom" - "What's the sitcom arc?"

## E — Executable steps
1. Establish the old stability (the status quo).
2. Introduce the disruption (the event that breaks stability).
3. Show the characters trying to restore stability (and failing).
4. Reach the new stability (the resolution).
5. Check: the new stability must be different from the old — or the same but with the audience wiser.

## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: arc that's about change — sitcom is about stability, not growth.
- **Trap**: disruption that's too big — the comedy is in the attempt to restore, not the restoration.
- **Trap**: new stability that's the same as old — the audience must feel something changed.
