# Sitcom Comic Engine

## R — Reading
The comic engine is a character trait applied consistently. The character does not need to try to be funny; their trait applied consistently is inherently comic. The engine is the *combination* of trait + situation — the trait is fuel, the situation is combustion.

## I — Interpretation
The engine works because audiences learn the character's pattern and anticipate variations. A comic engine character doesn't need to *try* to be funny — their trait applied consistently is inherently comic.

## A1 — Application
For shortform: choose ONE trait that is comic in itself (obsession, incompetence, extreme honesty). Apply it consistently — the comedy is watching the trait produce different outcomes in different contexts.

## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Design a sitcom character" - "This character is boring" - "How do I make this character funny?"

## E — Executable steps
1. Choose one comic trait (flaw, obsession, blind spot).
2. State how the trait manifests in behavior.
3. Apply the trait to 3 different contexts.
4. Check the trait's escalation potential.
5. If the trait produces only one kind of joke, narrow or expand it.

## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: trait too broad — "he's crazy" has no pattern.
- **Trap**: trait that disappears under pressure — the engine must work even when stressed.
- **Trap**: character who knows they're funny — self-awareness kills the engine.
