﻿# Callback

## R — Reading
Callback repeats a motif with variation; closes the loop. Carter's model: the audience laughs because they remember the earlier reference. The callback works because it rewards attention — the audience feels smart for catching it.

## I — Interpretation
The callback works because it's a delayed payoff — the setup is earlier, the payoff is later. The callback fails when it's too obvious (the audience saw it coming) or too obscure (the audience didn't catch it).

## A1 — Application
For shortform: place the callback at the end. The callback should be a variation on the earlier setup, not a repetition.

## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Write a callback" - "Close the loop" - "Reference the earlier joke"

## E — Executable steps
1. Identify the earlier setup.
2. Write the callback — a variation on the setup.
3. Check: is the callback clear enough to be recognized?
4. Check: is the callback different enough to be a variation?
5. Verify: the callback should feel earned, not forced.

## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: callback that's too obvious — the audience saw it coming.
- **Trap**: callback that's too obscure — the audience didn't catch it.
- **Trap**: callback that's just a repetition — it must be a variation.
