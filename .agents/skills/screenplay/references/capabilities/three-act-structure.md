# Three-Act Structure

## R — Reading
Field's paradigm: every screenplay divides into three acts. Act I — Set-Up (establishes world, characters, inciting incident). Act II — Confrontation (the longest act; the protagonist faces escalating obstacles). Act III — Resolution (the conflict is resolved; the protagonist either achieves or fails their dramatic need). The paradigm uses **Plot Points** — incidents that hook the action and spin it in another direction — as hinges between acts.

## I — Interpretation
The three-act structure is not just a beat pattern; it's a *pressure model*. Act I creates the want, Act II applies the pressure, Act III resolves it. The Plot Points are the moments where the protagonist's situation fundamentally changes — not just "something happens" but "the direction reverses."

## A1 — Application
For shortform: the three-act is compressed. Act I = establish the comic rule (0–10s). Act II = escalate (10–45s). Act III = payoff + resolution (45–60s). Plot Points in shortform = the moments where the comic rule shifts or intensifies.

## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Structure this story"
- "What's the three-act structure for this?"
- "How do I divide this into acts?"

## E — Executable steps
1. Write the protagonist's dramatic need (what they must achieve).
2. Write the inciting incident — the event that disturbs the balance.
3. Write Plot Point I — the event that pushes the protagonist into confrontation.
4. Write Plot Point II — the event that pushes toward resolution.
5. Write the climax — the moment the need is achieved or definitively fails.
6. Check: each act must have a distinct purpose; the plot points must genuinely change direction.

## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: inciting incident that's just the opening event — it must be the *cause* of the story.
- **Trap**: plot point that's just a climax — it must change direction, not just intensify.
- **Trap**: Act II that's just "more of the same" — confrontation must escalate obstacles.
- **Not** for sketch comedy's rule-based structure; three-act is for causal narrative.
