﻿# Conflict Chain

## R — Reading
Field's action-reaction chain: "Your character acts, and somebody reacts. Action-reaction, reaction-action — your story always moves toward that Plot Point at the end of each act." Each scene produces a new conflict that drives the next; scenes don't just happen, they *cause* the next scene.

## I — Interpretation
The chain works because drama is causal: A does X, so B does Y, which forces A to do Z. Break the chain — a scene that doesn't cause the next — and the story stalls. The chain's integrity is the audience's sense of inevitability.

## A1 — Application
For shortform: every beat should cause the next. If a beat is removed and the story still works, that beat wasn't necessary. Check each beat: does it force the next beat?

## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Why does this story feel slow?"
- "This scene doesn't lead anywhere"
- "Connect these scenes"

## E — Executable steps
1. Write each beat's action in one sentence.
2. For each beat, write the reaction it produces.
3. Check: does that reaction become the next beat's action?
4. If a beat doesn't produce a reaction, cut it or merge it.
5. Verify the chain ends at the plot point.

## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: coincidental causality — the next scene happens *because* of the previous, not just *after* it.
- **Trap**: parallel scenes — two scenes that don't affect each other.
- **Trap**: broken chain — a scene that produces no reaction.
