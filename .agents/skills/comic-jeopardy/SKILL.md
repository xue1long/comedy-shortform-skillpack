﻿# Jeopardy / Stakes Engine

## R — Reading
Jeopardy is the audience's reason to care: something can be lost. Without it, even the cleverest tools produce silence. Vorhaus's rule: the greater the jeopardy, the better the comedy. To raise stakes, increase the **price of failure** (what the hero loses if they fail) and the **prize for success** (what they gain if they succeed). Bias toward dynamic over logic — in comedy, the more outrageous choice is usually the right one.

## I — Interpretation
Jeopardy works on two axes: material (what can be lost) and emotional (how the hero feels about it). The "price of failure" answers "what goes wrong if they lose?"; the "prize for success" answers "what do they gain?". The rule is asymmetric — comedy prefers the dynamic (less predictable) choice.

## A1 — Application
- Diagnose "the sketch feels flat" — usually missing jeopardy.
- Raise stakes mid-scene when the bit is running long.
- Check whether the character's failure actually matters.
- Stack with comic-toolbox — tension-and-release works best when there's something to lose.

## A2 — Trigger signals
- "This is funny but I don't care"
- "Raise the stakes"
- "Why doesn't the audience care about this character?"
- "The payoff feels hollow — what's missing?"

## E — Executable steps
1. State the hero's outer need (what they want).
2. Name the price of failure (what's lost if they fail).
3. Name the prize for success (what's gained if they succeed).
4. Check asymmetry — does the dynamic choice lead to a more comic outcome?
5. If jeopardy is weak, raise the price (the failure is worse) or the prize (the win is bigger).

## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: jeopardy with no clear loser — "the world could end" is too vague.
- **Trap**: raising stakes without connecting to the character's perspective — it's loud but not funny.
- **Trap**: dynamic choice that violates the premise — the choice must still be a comic extension.
- **Not** the same as drama — comedy jeopardy is usually *personal* and *fixable* in the last beat.
