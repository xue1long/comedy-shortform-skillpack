﻿# Comic Throughline — Seven-Beat Spine

## R — Reading
A throughline is the story spine that runs through every scene and produces the comic ending. Vorhaus's seven beats:
1. Ordinary World / Ordinary Need (hero's routine desire)
2. Inciting Incident / Union Forced (hero meets the comic situation)
3. Complications / Things Fall Apart (the plan fails)
4. Hero Hits Bottom (the lowest point — the hero seems defeated)
5. Monkey Wrench (the comic accident that displaces loyalty)
6. Reunion / New Stability (the hero re-enters on new terms)
7. Double Win (outer need fulfilled + inner need resolved — the comic payoff)

## I — Interpretation
Outer need = what the hero wants; inner need = what the hero needs to become. The Monkey Wrench is the moment loyalty shifts — the hero's *want* is replaced by the *need*. Double Win resolves both: the hero gets what they wanted *because* they became what they needed.

## A1 — Application
Use for any narrative comic piece — 60s video, 3-scene short, sketch with progression. Especially useful when the sketch has a "point" beyond a single joke.

## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Build the story spine for this premise"
- "This feels like a one-joke bit — how do I make it a story?"
- "Does my sketch have a satisfying ending?"
- "Where does the hero's desire hit bottom?"

## E — Executable steps
1. State the hero's outer need (what they literally want).
2. State the hero's inner need (what they actually need to become).
3. Map the seven beats on one line: Ordinary → Union → Falls Apart → Hits Bottom → Monkey Wrench → Reunion → Double Win.
4. For each beat, identify which comic tool (from comic-toolbox) is doing the work.
5. Check the Monkey Wrench — it must genuinely displace the hero's loyalty, not just delay the plot.

## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: Monkey Wrench that's just "the plan fails again" — it must change the hero's *direction*.
- **Trap**: Double Win that's only outer — inner need must also resolve.
- **Trap**: seven beats forced into a 30s sketch — merge beats 1–2 for short forms.
- **Not** for joke sketches with no progression; the toolbox alone handles those.
