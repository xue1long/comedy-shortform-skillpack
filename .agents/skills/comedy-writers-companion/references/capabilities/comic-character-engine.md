# Comic Character Engine
## R — Reading
A comic engine is a character trait applied consistently. Vorhaus's model: a character with a single exaggerated trait (or comic flaw) will generate comedy in any scene because the trait produces predictable, escalating reactions. The engine is the *combination* of trait + situation — the trait is fuel, the situation is combustion.
## I — Interpretation
The engine works because audiences learn the character's pattern and anticipate variations. A comic engine character doesn't need to *try* to be funny — their trait applied consistently is inherently comic. The engine fails when the trait is too broad (no pattern to anticipate) or too narrow (exhausted in one scene).
## A1 — Application
For shortform: choose ONE trait that is comic in itself (obsession, incompetence, extreme honesty, social blindness). Apply it consistently — the comedy is watching the trait produce different outcomes in different contexts.
## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Design a comic character"
- "This character is boring"
- "How do I make this character funny?"
## E — Executable steps
1. Choose one comic trait (flaw, obsession, blind spot).
2. State how the trait manifests in behavior.
3. Apply the trait to 3 different contexts (home, work, social).
4. Check the trait's escalation potential — can it be pushed further?
5. If the trait produces only one kind of joke, narrow or expand it.
## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: trait too broad — "he's crazy" has no pattern to exploit.
- **Trap**: trait that disappears under pressure — the engine must work even when the character is stressed.
- **Trap**: character who knows they're funny — self-awareness kills the engine.
