# Comic Premise (Kaplan)

## R — Reading
Kaplan's comic premise is the lie the closed universe operates on. Where Carter's premise is the *world's* belief, Kaplan's premise is the **closed universe's operating assumption** — the internal logic of the comic world. Kaplan's signature: combine premise with the Comic Equation (how funny = truth × pain × distance). Examples: in a Seinfeld episode, the universe's lie might be "the smallest social violations are crimes"; the comedy follows from that closed logic being applied consistently.

Kaplan's comic universe has three legs: premise (the lie), character (the trait that engages the lie), engine (the mechanism that produces scenes from the lie).

## I — Interpretation
Kaplan's premise is more specific than Carter's. The "closed universe" means the lie doesn't have to be the real world's belief — it can be a deliberately constructed comic premise that the characters and world consistently act on. The comedy comes from the engine producing variations on the lie across many scenes.

## A1 — Application
For shortform: state the lie as a closed universe operating rule. The comedy is the rule being applied to different situations consistently.

Use when: you need a premise that supports multiple scenes or sketches with the same engine.
Don't use when: the conflict is a single scene's escalation (use comic-premise-carter instead).

## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Build me a recurring engine around a lie"
- "What assumption does this comic universe operate on?"
- "Combine premise + comic equation"

## E — Executable steps
1. State the closed universe's lie as an operating rule.
2. Check: does the rule apply to multiple situations consistently?
3. Identify the comic engine (how the rule generates scenes).
4. Apply the Comic Equation (truth × pain × distance) to verify each scene's humor.
5. Test: does each scene feel like a *variation* on the premise, not a separate joke?

## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: premise that only works once — Kaplan's premise must support multiple scenes.
- **Trap**: premise that's the world's belief (that's Carter, not Kaplan).
- **Trap**: premise without closed universe — premise must be the operating rule, not an external truth.
- **Distinguish from Carter**: Kaplan's lie is the *closed-universe's* operating rule; Carter's lie is the *world's* general belief.
