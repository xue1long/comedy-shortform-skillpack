# Straight / Wavy Line
## R — Reading
The straight line is the comic anchor — the character (or element) that represents normal, reasonable, expected behavior. The wavy line is the comic disturbance — the character (or element) that deviates, disrupts, surprises. Kaplan's focus model: every comic scene needs a straight line for the audience to orient against; the wavy line is what we watch.
## I — Interpretation
The straight line works because comedy is deviation from expectation — without a baseline of normal, the deviation is invisible. The wavy line works because it's what the audience is watching — they're not watching the straight line, they're watching the wavy line's effect on the straight line.
## A1 — Application
For shortform: identify which character is the anchor (who the audience identifies with). The wavy line is the character whose behavior surprises. Don't make the straight line a killjoy — their normalcy is the engine.
## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Who should be the straight man?"
- "This scene is chaos — no anchor"
- "How do I make this funnier?"
## E — Executable steps
1. Identify the situation.
2. Assign the straight line (who is normal?).
3. Assign the wavy line (who deviates?).
4. Check: does the straight line have a reason to be there (not just a killjoy)?
5. Check: does the wavy line produce consistent deviation (not random)?
6. Write beats where the wavy line tests the straight line's normalcy.
## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: straight line as killjoy — they must have their own want.
- **Trap**: wavy line that's random — deviation must be character-driven.
- **Trap**: no straight line — chaos has no baseline for surprise.
