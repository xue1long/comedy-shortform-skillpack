# Comic Hero
## R — Reading
The comic hero is defined by three qualities: **winning** (they want something specific), **positive action** (they pursue it), **active emotion** (they feel it now, not backstory). Kaplan's rule: the hero must NOT try to be funny — they take themselves seriously. The comedy comes from the gap between their serious pursuit and the world's response.
## I — Interpretation
The hero works because they give the audience a point of entry — we identify with their want even when the want is absurd. The hero fails when they're a joke (self-aware, self-deprecating) or when they have no want (passive, reactive).
## A1 — Application
For shortform: write the hero's want in one sentence. Everything they say and do must serve that want. The comedy is watching them pursue it seriously while the world disagrees.
## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Design a comedy protagonist"
- "The hero isn't working"
- "How do I make this character comic?"
## E — Executable steps
1. Write the hero's want in one sentence (specific, immediate).
2. Check: is the want positive (they pursue) not reactive (they respond)?
3. Check: does the hero take themselves seriously?
4. Check: does the world disagree (negative push)?
5. If the hero is self-aware, make them blind to their own absurdity.
## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: hero who's trying to be funny — they must believe they're serious.
- **Trap**: want that's too small — the want must be worth fighting for.
- **Trap**: no active emotion — the hero must feel the want NOW.
