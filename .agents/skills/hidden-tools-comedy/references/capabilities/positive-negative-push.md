# Positive / Negative Push
## R — Reading
Positive push = what the hero pursues; negative push = what the world pushes back. Kaplan's model: comedy is the collision between these forces. The hero's positive push is their want; the negative push is the obstacle, the world, the other characters' wants. The comedy comes from the gap between what the hero intends and what the world allows.
## I — Interpretation
The push model works because it externalizes comedy — the hero isn't funny in themselves; the collision is. If there's no negative push, the hero wins too easily and there's no comedy. If the negative push is too strong, the hero can't move and there's no story.
## A1 — Application
For shortform: identify the hero's want (positive push) and the obstacle (negative push). The scene is the collision — write it so the hero's action makes the obstacle worse, not better.
## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Why is this scene flat?"
- "What's the comic force here?"
- "Map the comedy dynamics"
## E — Executable steps
1. Write the hero's want (positive push).
2. Identify the obstacle (negative push).
3. Check: does the hero's action make the obstacle worse?
4. Check: does the obstacle escalate?
5. The payoff is when the collision produces the unexpected — the hero gets what they wanted in the worst way.
## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: obstacle that's too weak — the hero wins too easily.
- **Trap**: obstacle that's too strong — the hero can't move.
- **Trap**: collision that resolves cleanly — comedy needs the unexpected payoff.
