﻿# Strong Comic Perspective + Funnel Character

## R — Reading
A comic character is driven by a Strong Comic Perspective — a world-view at variance with normal reality that consistently produces funny choices. The character is a **funnel**: Strong Comic Perspective + Flaws (distance) + Humanity (audience connection) + Exaggeration (all pushed to endpoint).

## I — Interpretation
The four-element formula makes character behavior predictable *and* comic. Strong Comic Perspective is the engine; Flaws separate the character from real people; Humanity pulls them back; Exaggeration takes everything to the extreme. "Funnel" means the three forces compress behavior into a narrow predictable channel.

## A1 — Application
Use when creating or diagnosing a character. Turn any weak character into a funnel by:
- Naming the perspective ("I always assume everyone is plotting against me")
- Assigning a flaw (lazy, vain, fearful)
- Assigning humanity (genuine want, genuine fear)
- Exaggerating each element to the endpoint.

## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Create a comic character for X"
- "This character isn't funny / predictable enough"
- "How do I write a character like Groucho / Michael Scott / etc.?"
- "Give me a flawed character for this sketch"

## E — Executable steps
1. Define the Strong Comic Perspective in one sentence ("They believe X").
2. Identify the character's flaw (what separates them from real people).
3. Identify the character's humanity (what connects them to the audience).
4. Exaggerate each element to its endpoint ("to the end of the line").
5. Verify the funnel: any action they take should be predictable from these three elements.

## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: perspective too vague ("quirky" is not a comic perspective).
- **Trap**: flaw without humanity — the character becomes a villain.
- **Trap**: humanity without perspective — the character is sympathetic but not comic.
- **Not** for ensemble casts; use one funnel per major character.
