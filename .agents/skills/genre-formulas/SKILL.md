﻿# Genre Formulas

## R — Reading
Blake's genre formulas: predictable comic patterns that audiences recognize. Fish-out-of-water, mistaken identity, role reversal, secret identity, time-travel. Each formula has a distinct comic engine.

## I — Interpretation
Genre works because audiences know the pattern — they can predict the beats, which makes the surprises funnier. The formula is the skeleton; the specific content is the flesh.

## A1 — Application
For shortform: choose a formula that fits your premise. The formula gives you the skeleton; your job is the specific jokes.

## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Choose a genre formula" - "What genre is this?" - "How do I make this genre-specific?"

## E — Executable steps
1. Identify your premise's core absurdity.
2. Match it to a genre formula (fish-out-of-water, mistaken identity, etc.).
3. Use the formula's beats as your skeleton.
4. Fill in the specific jokes.
5. Check: does the formula's engine match your premise?

## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: formula that doesn't match the premise — the skeleton won't support the jokes.
- **Trap**: formula that's too rigid — the skeleton must serve the comedy, not constrain it.
- **Not** for non-genre comedy where the premise is original.
