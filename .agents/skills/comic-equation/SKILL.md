﻿# The Comic Equation
## R — Reading
Kaplan's comic equation: comedy is about an ordinary person struggling against insurmountable odds without many of the required skills, yet never giving up hope. The audience laughs because they recognize the struggle — not because the situation is inherently funny. The equation diagnoses: if the protagonist is skilled, the odds are beatable, or they give up, it's not comedy.
## I — Interpretation
The equation works because comedy is empathy + distance — we laugh at the gap between what the hero thinks they can do and what they actually can. If the hero is competent, the gap closes and comedy dies. The equation is diagnostic: apply it to any scene to check if the comic engine is present.
## A1 — Application
For shortform: check every scene — does the hero have a chance? Do they have the skills? If yes to either, rewrite to reduce their capability or raise the odds.
## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Is this scene funny?"
- "Why isn't this comedy working?"
- "Diagnose this scene's comedy"
## E — Executable steps
1. Identify the protagonist.
2. Check: are they ordinary (not skilled)?
3. Check: are the odds insurmountable?
4. Check: do they have any of the required skills?
5. Check: do they give up hope?
6. If any check fails, rewrite to restore the equation.
## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: hero who is secretly competent — the equation requires genuine skill deficit.
- **Trap**: odds that are beatable — insurmountable means the hero CAN'T win.
- **Trap**: hopelessness without action — the hero must still try.
