# Sketch Comedy — Nine-Step Construction

## R — Reading
A sketch is a single-rule comic form: establish a comic rule, escalate it, and pay it off. Vorhaus's nine steps:
1. Strong comic character (with a specific perspective)
2. Force of opposition (something that resists the perspective)
3. Force a union (put them in the same space)
4. Escalate conflict toward the emotional core
5. Raise the stakes
6. Push the limits (the character goes further than expected)
7. Find a winner (decide who wins the conflict)
8. Change the frame (the payoff recontextualizes everything)
9. Coda / callback (optional — a final frame-shift or running-gag closure)

## I — Interpretation
The nine-step skeleton separates "situation" (steps 1–3), "development" (4–6), and "resolution" (7–9). Skipping step 2 (no opposition) produces a monologue; skipping step 7 (no winner) produces an anticlimax; skipping step 8 produces a flat payoff.

## A1 — Application
Use for any 30–90s sketch or short-form comic piece. For 60s sketches: steps 1–3 ≈ 0–15s, 4–6 ≈ 15–45s, 7–9 ≈ 45–60s.

## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Write a 60-second sketch"
- "Structure this sketch idea"
- "Why isn't my sketch working?" (usually a missing beat)
- "How do I escalate this comic premise?"

## E — Executable steps
1. State the comic premise (one falsifiable sentence).
2. Choose the comic perspective (from comic-perspective).
3. Identify the force of opposition (the "wall" that resists the perspective).
4. Force the union (same room, same task, same goal).
5. Escalate with at least two variations of the rule (Tension and Release + Rule of Three).
6. Pick the winner — who satisfies their perspective vs who is defeated.
7. Land the payoff at the last 5–10s (the frame-shift that recontextualizes the whole piece).
8. Add a coda only if it doesn't delay the payoff.

## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: nine steps is a checklist, not a straitjacket — a 30s sketch may merge steps.
- **Trap**: escalation without perspective — the conflict is loud but not comic.
- **Trap**: payoff without frame-shift — "the character was right all along" is not a payoff.
- **Not** for multi-scene or serialized work; sketch is a single-scene form.
