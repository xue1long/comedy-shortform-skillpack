# The Comic Toolbox — Overview

**Source:** *The Comic Toolbox* by John Vorhaus
**Bundle:** bundle.comic-toolbox
**Router:** `comic-toolbox`

## Core model

The book teaches comedy as a craft of *truth and pain*: comedy is truth
exaggerated, and every laugh begins with something that hurts. The two
structural halves of the book are:

1. **Comic premise** — the gap between comic reality and real reality.
   Premises are falsifiable one-sentence claims ("people who talk to their
   pets are smarter than people who don't"). A premise earns its keep when it
   can be proven wrong — the gap is where the jokes live.
2. **The Comic Toolbox** — ten named tools that turn any dry beat into a
   comic one (clash of context, wildly inappropriate response, law of comic
   opposites, tension and release, position the payoff, truth/lie to comic
   effect, rule of three, jokoid, doorbell effect, running gag/callback).

## Reading path

Start with Ch.1 (truth and pain) → Ch.3 (premise) → Ch.5 (the ten tools) →
Ch.13 (sketch) → Ch.7 (throughline). Ch.12 (sitcom arc) is for ensemble work.

## Scope boundary

The book does not teach stand-up, improv, or visual comedy directly, and its
pre-1994 examples are Western/sitcom-centric — it does not cover short-form
web drama. For short-form beats beyond sketch, route to
`comedy-writers-companion` (escalation) or `comedy-bible` (joke craft).
