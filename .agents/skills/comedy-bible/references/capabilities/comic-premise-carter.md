# Comic Premise (Carter)

## R — Reading
Carter's premise is the lie the world believes. Carter's distinction: the premise is what the world assumes to be true, not what one character wants. Examples Carter uses: "the world is a meritocracy" (the lie) vs. arbitrary reality; "love conquers all" (the lie) vs. messy relationships. The premise works because it is **falsifiable** — the audience knows it's false, and the comedy is watching the world act as if it's true.

Carter's test for a working premise: state it in one sentence; if you can prove it wrong, it has a gap. If you can't prove it wrong, it's actually true and has no comedy.

## I — Interpretation
The premise is distinct from "theme" or "logline" — it is the *assertion* the comedy tests. Carter's signature: the premise is the **world's lie**, not the character's. The comedy comes from how the world acts on the lie. This contrasts with Kaplan, where the lie is **the closed universe's** operating assumption.

## A1 — Application
For shortform: state the lie as a world-level assertion. The comedy is the world proving itself wrong.

Use when: a sketch or scene needs a falsifiable world-belief to drive the comedy.
Don't use when: the conflict is a character's internal contradiction (use art-of-character instead).

## A2 — Trigger signals
- "What's the lie this comedy assumes?"
- "Is this premise strong enough to carry 60 seconds?"
- "Make this premise more comic"

## E — Executable steps
1. State the lie in one sentence (the world's false assumption).
2. Check: is it falsifiable? Can the audience see it's false?
3. Check: does the world (not just one character) believe it?
4. Write the collision — the moment the lie meets reality.
5. The payoff is when the lie produces the unexpected consequence.

## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: premise that is actually true — no collision, no comedy.
- **Trap**: premise that's just a character's want — that's a desire, not a premise.
- **Trap**: premise too broad ("life is hard") — no falsifiable gap.
- **Distinguish from Kaplan**: Carter's lie is the *world's* belief; Kaplan's lie is the *closed-universe's* operating assumption.
