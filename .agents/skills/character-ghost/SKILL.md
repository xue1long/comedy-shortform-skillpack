﻿# Character Ghost
## R — Reading
A ghost is a past wound that shapes present desire. Corbett's model: the ghost is the wound (trauma, loss, failure) that created the character's current want/need structure. The ghost doesn't need to be revealed — it needs to be *felt* — the audience senses the wound through the character's behavior.
## I — Interpretation
The ghost works because it gives the character's desire weight — we understand WHY they want what they want. The ghost fails when it's explained (backstory dump) or when it's too neat (the wound perfectly explains the desire).
## A1 — Application
For shortform: identify the character's wound. Don't explain it — show it through behavior. The ghost is the engine that makes the character's desire feel necessary, not arbitrary.
## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Why does this character want this?"
- "Add backstory"
- "Make this character deeper"
## E — Executable steps
1. Identify the wound (what happened before the story).
2. Check: does the wound produce the character's current want?
3. Write the scene where the character's behavior reveals the wound.
4. Check: is the ghost felt, not explained?
5. The payoff is when the audience understands the wound without being told.
## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: explained ghost — telling the wound kills the mystery.
- **Trap**: neat ghost — the wound perfectly explains everything.
- **Trap**: ghost that doesn't matter — the wound must produce the current desire.
