# Scene-Based Character Revelation
## R — Reading
Reveal character through action under pressure, not description. Corbett's model: character is revealed when the character is forced to choose under pressure — the choice reveals their true nature. The scene is the pressure; the action is the revelation.
## I — Interpretation
Scene-based revelation works because audiences believe what they see under pressure, not what they're told in description. The revelation fails when the pressure is insufficient (easy choices reveal nothing) or when the action is ambiguous (the audience can't tell what the choice means).
## A1 — Application
For shortform: write a scene where the character is forced to choose. The choice reveals their desire, contradiction, or ghost — not through explanation, but through action.
## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Reveal this character"
- "Show don't tell this character"
- "Make this character's nature clear"
## E — Executable steps
1. Identify the character's key trait.
2. Write a scene that forces the character to choose.
3. Check: is the pressure sufficient (high stakes)?
4. Check: does the choice reveal the trait?
5. The revelation is in the action, not in dialogue about it.
## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: insufficient pressure — easy choices reveal nothing.
- **Trap**: ambiguous action — the audience can't tell what the choice means.
- **Trap**: revelation that's announced — telling kills the reveal.
