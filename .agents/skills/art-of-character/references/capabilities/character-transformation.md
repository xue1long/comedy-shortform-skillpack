# Character Transformation
## R — Reading
Transformation: growth through action, not realization. Corbett's model: the character doesn't "learn a lesson" — they act differently because the cost of their old behavior has become too high. Transformation is visible in behavior, not in speech. The arc is the gap between the character's opening behavior and their closing behavior.
## I — Interpretation
Transformation works because audiences believe what they see, not what they're told. The transformation fails when it's announced ("I've learned my lesson") or when it's unearned (the character changes without sufficient pressure).
## A1 — Application
For shortform: the transformation is in the last beat — the character does something they couldn't do at the start. Don't announce it; show it.
## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Write a character arc"
- "Show growth"
- "End the story with transformation"
## E — Executable steps
1. Write the character's opening behavior (the "before").
2. Identify the pressure that makes the old behavior too costly.
3. Write the closing behavior (the "after") — same situation, different action.
4. Check: is the transformation earned (sufficient pressure)?
5. Don't announce the transformation — show it.
## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: announced transformation — telling kills the arc.
- **Trap**: unearned transformation — not enough pressure.
- **Trap**: no transformation — the character ends where they started.
