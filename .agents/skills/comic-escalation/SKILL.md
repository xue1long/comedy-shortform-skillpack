﻿# Comic Escalation
## R — Reading
Escalation is predictable variation on a comic rule. Each beat tests the rule under a new condition — escalation works when the audience can predict the pattern but not the specific variation. Vorhaus's rule of thumb: escalation beats are about *degree*, not *kind* — pushing the rule further, not changing it.
## I — Interpretation
Escalation is the engine that keeps a comic premise alive across a scene. Without escalation, comedy is a single beat repeated; with it, the premise generates multiple jokes from the same engine. The rule-of-three is the minimal escalation (two setups, one break); longform needs 4-7 escalations.
## A1 — Application
For shortform: identify the comic rule, then ask "what's the most extreme version of this rule?" — that's your last beat. Work backward to find the intermediate escalations that make the extreme feel earned.
## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Escalate this bit"
- "This joke only works once"
- "How do I make this funnier?"
## E — Executable steps
1. State the comic rule.
2. Identify the rule's variable (who/what/how).
3. Escalate by degree: more extreme, more characters, higher stakes.
4. The final beat breaks the pattern — the rule applied to itself or pushed to absurdity.
5. Check: each beat must be *more* than the last, not just *different*.
## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: escalation that goes in circles — same degree, different decoration.
- **Trap**: breaking the rule too early — the last beat should be the one that can't be topped.
- **Trap**: escalation that loses the audience — if the premise is forgotten, the escalation is too big a jump.
