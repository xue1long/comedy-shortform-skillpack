# Dialogue Misdirection
## R — Reading
Misdirected dialogue: the audience is made to look one way while the truth walks by another. Chiarella's toolkit: keep answers late or absent; let characters answer the question they wish was asked; use the false triangle (a third listener who interprets wrongly); interpolations that seem to digress but plant the payoff. Misdirection works because audiences assume dialogue answers questions — break that assumption.
## I — Interpretation
Misdirection is the engine of both mystery and comedy: comedy misdirection ends in a laugh (the misread is revealed), drama misdirection ends in dread or release. The craft skill is *fairness* — the audience must feel the answer was always there, not that the writer cheated.
## A1 — Application
For 60s shortform: place the misdirect in the first 15s (a question that gets the "wrong" answer) and pay it off in the last 10s. Misdirection + Rule of Three: two straight answers then the comic misread.
## A2 — Trigger signals
- "Make this reveal land harder"
- "The audience sees the twist coming"
- "How do I plant a payoff without it looking planted?"
## E — Executable steps
1. Write the straight Q&A version of the exchange.
2. Identify which answer carries the payoff.
3. Move that answer at least one beat late — insert a misread, an evasion, or an answer-to-a-different-question.
4. Check fairness: reread — was the true answer always planted?
5. Time the reveal at 80–90% of scene length.
## B — Boundary / common failures
- **Trap**: withholding answers the audience never asked — misdirection needs a live question first.
- **Trap**: cheating reveals (information the audience couldn't have inferred).
- **Trap**: misdirection that only obscures — comedy misdirection must recontextualize, not just hide.
